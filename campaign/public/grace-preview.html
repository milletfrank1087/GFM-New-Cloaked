Hi everyone, thank you so much for taking a moment to read our story.

Our daughter Grace is 21 years old and in her third year studying Medicine at the University of Manchester. For as long as she can remember, all she ever wanted was to become a doctor and care for patients. But in April this year, our entire world was turned upside down when Grace collapsed during a clinical lecture after suffering a sudden, unprovoked seizure.

She was rushed to the Manchester Centre for Clinical Neurosciences at Salford Royal Hospital. After an emergency MRI, doctors discovered a 4.2 cm tumour in her right frontal lobe. Within days, she had surgery and the pathology results came back with the diagnosis every family dreads: Glioblastoma Multiforme (WHO Grade IV). To make matters harder, molecular tests revealed her tumour has an unmethylated MGMT promoter, which means standard chemotherapy has very limited effectiveness against it.

"I wanted to become a doctor to help people. Now I'm learning what it means to be a patient — and how unbearable it is to know that a treatment exists that could save you, but you can't access it. Every donation isn't just money — it's time. Time I get to keep living. If I make it through this, I promise to dedicate my life to neuro-oncology, so no one else has to go through what I'm going through."
— Grace Clarke

Where we are now & standard treatment limits

Grace has shown unbelievable courage over the past four months. She underwent brain surgery at Salford Royal on 10th May 2026, followed by the Stupp protocol: six weeks of concurrent daily radiotherapy and chemotherapy. The NHS doctors and nurses have been extraordinary, and we cannot thank them enough for their care and dedication.

However, standard NHS treatment for glioblastoma has reached its limits. Because recurrence with this type of brain tumour is almost certain and fast, her neuro-oncology team explained that our best hope to give Grace long-term survival is dendritic cell immunotherapy — a specialised, cutting-edge treatment available at certified oncology centres in Germany (such as IOZK in Cologne).

The Hope: Dendritic Cell Immunotherapy in Germany

The immunotherapy in Germany takes Grace’s own immune cells from her blood, trains them in a specialised laboratory with antigens from her tumour tissue, and re-infuses them so her immune system actively attacks microscopic cancer cells. A recent clinical study published in Human Vaccines & Immunotherapeutics demonstrated a 68% increase in median overall survival for glioblastoma patients receiving dendritic cell vaccines.

Because this personalised immunotherapy is considered advanced and experimental, it is not funded by the NHS or NICE in the UK. The clinic in Germany has reviewed Grace's scans from Salford Royal and confirmed she is an eligible candidate to begin treatment in September 2026.

Transparent Cost Breakdown

The full protocol involves 4 to 6 cycles over the next 12 months, along with specialised proton therapy and essential medical travel and accommodation, coming to a total cost of £90,000:

  • £48,000 — 4 to 6 cycles of personalized dendritic cell vaccine preparation and clinical administration in Germany
  • £22,000 — Stereotactic proton beam therapy protocol
  • £8,000 — Next-generation molecular immuno-sequencing and monitoring
  • £9,000 — 12 months of travel, logistics and medical accommodation in Germany
  • £3,000 — Reserve for unforeseen medical prescriptions and care

As a family, we are putting everything we have toward this, but £90,000 is simply impossible for us alone. Grace has so much life ahead of her, so much she still wants to give to the world as a future doctor.

If you can donate anything at all — whether it's £10, £25 or whatever you can manage — or simply share this page with your friends, family and social networks, you are giving Grace a real fighting chance.

From the bottom of our hearts, thank you for your kindness, love and prayers.

Emma, David, and Grace Clarke x
