## Primary text

In an instant, everything changed.

On the left, Grace: 21, a medical student at the University of Manchester, dreaming of becoming a doctor. On the right, her reality today: Grace is fighting a grade IV brain tumour.

In June, mid-lecture, Grace had a seizure. Scans at Salford Royal found a 4.2 cm mass in her right frontal lobe. She was 21 and had glioblastoma, the most aggressive malignant brain tumour there is. Median survival on standard treatment is 12–15 months.

Let's be clear: her surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy at Salford Royal were provided by the NHS, free. That part is covered.

What is NOT available on the NHS is dendritic cell immunotherapy in Germany. A treatment that clinical studies show can increase survival by 68%. It is classed as an "innovative therapy", so no public funding reaches it. We need £90,000.

In her own words: "I wanted to become a doctor to help people. Now I'm learning what it means to be the patient — and how unbearable it is to know a treatment exists that could save you, but you can't reach it."

Every pound is time. Please don't let her face this alone.
